## Pricing architecture

| Tier | Price | Gate |
|---|---|---|
| Free | $0 | 250 issues (hard stop — issue creation halts), 2 teams, unlimited members |
| Basic | $10/user/mo, billed yearly | Unlimited issues, up to 5 teams, admin roles |
| Business | $16/user/mo, billed yearly | Unlimited teams, Insights, private teams, guests, priority support |
| Enterprise | Contact sales | SAML SSO, SCIM, API rate limits, invoice/PO billing, migration support |

Monthly billing exists at a higher per-seat rate; neither source published the monthly numbers.
[aiproductivity.ai, verified Aug 2026](https://aiproductivity.ai/blog/linear-pricing/) ·
[usecarly, Jul 2026](https://www.usecarly.com/blog/linear-pricing/) ·
[UsagePricing, Jul 2026](https://www.usagepricing.com/blueprint/linear)

**The metric:** pure per-seat, workspace-wide (not per-team). UsagePricing: "no usage meters,
overage charges, or token-based billing" on the subscription itself. ([UsagePricing](https://www.usagepricing.com/blueprint/linear))

## The AI exception (new this quarter)

Linear shipped **Coding Sessions** on 2026-06-11 — described as "its first usage-metered feature,"
billed from a workspace-level prepaid AI-credit balance. Launch promo: $20 × unsuspended users.
Default model at launch: Claude Opus 4.8. No per-seat AI surcharge was added to any plan.
([UsagePricing activity log, 2026-06-11](https://www.usagepricing.com/blueprint/activity/linear-2026-06-11-coding-sessions))

Current metered rates:

- Coding session sandbox: **$0.25 per 20-minute block**
- Coding session model tokens: **provider rates, no markup**
- Loop run (no coding): **~$0.07–$0.20**
- Credits roll over across billing cycles, **expire 12 months** from purchase, non-refundable
  ([usecarly, Linear AI](https://www.usecarly.com/blog/linear-ai/))

A further pricing change landed **2026-08-20/26**: flat Coding Sessions price tiers replaced by
"transparent token pass-through." Details not retrievable this run — the changelog entry is
`linear.app/changelog/2026-08-20-coding-environments`. ([UsagePricing](https://www.usagepricing.com/blueprint/linear))

**Sources disagree on two points:**

1. *Which tier unlocks Coding Sessions.* UsagePricing says Basic+; usecarly says Business+.
2. *Whether plans include credits.* The launch log records a promotional credit grant; usecarly
   states flatly "no Linear plan includes AI credits. Not Free, not Business, not Enterprise" —
   credits are opt-in, and a workspace that never adds funds is never charged.

Agents themselves are **not billed as seats** — the Agent platform, Triage Intelligence and Code
Intelligence are bundled into the seat price. ([usecarly](https://www.usecarly.com/blog/linear-ai/))

## Competitor comparison — Jira / Atlassian

Atlassian prices in **banded volume rates**, not a flat per-seat fee:

- Standard: $9.05/user/mo (1–100 users) → $6.40 (251–1,000) → $2.45 (90,000+). Blended ~$7.91 at 300 seats.
- Premium: blended ~$14.54 at 300 seats.
- Rovo AI credits bundled by tier: 25 / 70 / 150 credits per user per month (Standard / Premium / Enterprise).
- Overage: **$0.01 per credit ($10 per 1,000)**. Rovo Dev: **$20/developer/mo** incl. 2,000 credits.
  ([UsagePricing – Atlassian](https://www.usagepricing.com/blueprint/atlassian))

Net: Linear's list price sits *above* Jira's blended rate at scale and bundles less AI allowance,
but carries no volume discount ladder and no credit meter on the subscription.

## Three questions today's session should answer

1. Linear's NRR jumped from ~145% to 177% in under a year while the price card stayed at $10/$16 —
   is that seat expansion inside existing accounts, tier migration Basic→Business, or the credit
   line showing up in retention? Each one implies a different business.
2. Coding Sessions pass tokens through at cost and charge $0.25 per 20-minute sandbox block. If
   agents become the primary way work gets done in Linear, does the seat still price the value —
   or is Linear metering the expensive thing at zero margin and charging for the cheap one?
3. Jira discounts to $2.45/seat at volume; Linear has no published ladder. What has to be true
   about who Linear sells to for a flat card to survive a 5,000-seat deal — and does Enterprise
   "contact sales" already mean it hasn't?
