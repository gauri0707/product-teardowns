## Pricing — teams

- Teams Standard **$40/user/mo monthly, $32/user/mo annual**; Teams Premium **$120 monthly / $96 annual**; Enterprise custom ([Improvements to Teams Pricing](https://cursor.com/blog/teams-pricing-june-2026), June 2026).
- That post split usage into **two pools**: first-party models (Auto, Composer 2.5) and third-party API access. Standard seats got "significantly more usage, with no change in cost." Premium is "5x the included usage of the Standard seat, at only 3x the cost," expected to cover a month of heavy use for **99% of users**. Effective immediately for new customers, **1 July 2026** for renewals.
- Stated reasoning: model selection is "the most important determinant of costs"; Composer 2.5 gives "frontier performance at a fraction of the cost."

## Three questions today's session should answer

1. Cursor sells seats, but its cost is set by which model a request routes to — a variable the buyer mostly can't see. Is "seat plus an unquantified pool" a deliberate choice, or a placeholder until routing is good enough to price on outcomes?
2. Enterprise seats are gross-margin positive and individual seats are not. Now that the compute owner also owns the company, what is the $20 Pro tier for — funnel, data, or a subsidy with no stated exit?
3. The margin turned when Cursor started routing to models it controls. At what point does preferring first-party models stop reading as an economics win and start reading as a product-quality tax the user pays?
